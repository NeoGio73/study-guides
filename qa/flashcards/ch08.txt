#c001 [redox 1] Term
  F: In organic chemistry, what counts as a reduction? What symbol is used for it?
  B: An increase in hydrogen content or a decrease in oxygen content. General symbol: [H].
  N: Example: carboxylic acid → aldehyde → primary alcohol are both reductions.
#c002 [redox 2] Term
  F: What counts as an oxidation? What symbol is used for it?
  B: An increase in oxygen content (or of any element more electronegative than carbon), or a decrease in hydrogen content. General symbol: [O].
#c003 [redox 3] Concept
  F: Arrange these from lowest to highest oxidation state.
  SF: [aceticacid:acetic acid] "·" [ethane:ethane] "·" [acetaldehyde:acetaldehyde] "·" [ethanol:ethanol]
  B: alkane < alcohol < aldehyde < carboxylic acid
  SB: [ethane:ethane (nocap)] --[O] / --> [ethanol:ethanol (nocap)] --[O] / --> [acetaldehyde:acetaldehyde (nocap)] --[O] / --> [aceticacid:acetic acid (nocap)]
  N: Each step to the right is an [O]; each step back to the left is an [H].
#c004 [redox 4] Concept
  F: Is this an oxidation or a reduction?
  SF: [propene:propene] --H_2, Pt / --> [propane:propane]
  B: Reduction. Two hydrogens are added and no oxygen. Catalytic hydrogenation is a reduction.
#c005 [redox 5] Concept
  F: Is this an oxidation or a reduction?
  SF: [propene:propene] --(1) OsO_4 / (2) NaHSO_3, H_2O--> [propanediol:1,2-propanediol]
  B: Oxidation. Two new C–O bonds form.
#c006 [redox 6] Concept
  F: Adding HBr to an alkene: oxidation, reduction, or neither?
  SF: [propene:propene (nocap)] --HBr / --> [bromo2p:2-bromopropane (nocap)]
  B: Neither, overall.
  N: One carbon gains a C–H bond (reduced) while the other gains a C–Br bond (oxidized), so the overall oxidation level is unchanged.
#c007 [hydrog 1] Reaction
  F: Predict the product.

  B: Propane — the unsaturated alkene becomes a saturated alkane.

#c008 [hydrog 2] Concept
  F: Pt, Pd and Ni are heterogeneous hydrogenation catalysts. What does that mean, and how do they work?
  B: They are finely divided, insoluble metals. H_2 and the alkene both adsorb onto the metal surface, and the two C–H bonds then form stepwise.
#c009 [hydrog 3] Term
  F: Define syn addition and anti addition.
  B: Syn: both new groups add to the same face of the double bond.Anti: the two groups add to opposite faces.

  B: cis-1,2-Dimethylcyclohexane.
  SB: [cisdmch:cis-1,2-dimethylcyclohexane]
  N: Syn addition: both H atoms are delivered from the catalyst surface to the same face, so the two methyl groups end up on the same side of the ring.
  3D: cisdmch cis-1,2-dimethylcyclohexane: both CH₃ groups on the same side None
#c011 [hydrog 5] Concept
  F: Why does partial hydrogenation of vegetable oil raise health concerns?
  B: It turns liquid oils into solid cooking fats, but it also isomerizes some cis C=C bonds to trans. These “trans fats” have been associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular disease.
#c012 [hx 1] Concept
  F: In an electrophilic addition, what role does the alkene play in step 1, and what does it become in step 2?
  B: Step 1: its loosely held π electrons make the alkene a nucleophile, which attacks H^+.Step 2: the resulting carbocation is an electrophile, attacked by the nucleophile X^–.
#c013 [hx 2] Reaction
  F: Predict the major product.
  SF: [propene:propene] --HBr / --> ?
  B: 2-Bromopropane (major). Only a little 1-bromopropane forms.
  SB: [bromo2p:2-bromopropane]
  N: H adds to the CH_2 end to give the more stable 2° carbocation; Br^– then bonds to that carbon.
#c014 [hx 3] Term
  F: State Markovnikov’s rule (classic version).
  B: When HX adds to an alkene, the H adds to the double-bond carbon that already has more hydrogens, so X ends up on the more substituted carbon.
#c015 [hx 4] Term
  F: State the modern version of Markovnikov’s rule.
  B: In the ionic addition of an unsymmetrical reagent to a double bond, the positive part of the reagent attaches to the carbon that produces the more stable carbocation intermediate.
#c016 [hx 5] Term
  F: What is a regioselective reaction?
  B: A reaction that could give two or more constitutional isomers but actually gives only one, or mostly one.
#c017 [hx 6] Concept
  F: In HX addition, which step is rate-determining? How do the two steps compare energetically?
  B: Step 1, forming the carbocation, is slow and highly endergonic: it is rate-determining. Step 2, capture by X^–, is fast and highly exergonic.
#c018 [hx 7] Concept
  F: Rank carbocation stability: methyl, 1°, 2°, 3°.
  B: 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl
  N: Alkyl groups donate electron density and help stabilize the positive charge.
#c019 [hx 8] Concept
  F: Why does the more stable carbocation also form faster?
  B: The transition state for step 1 resembles the carbocation, so anything that stabilizes the carbocation also stabilizes that transition state. The 2° or 3° path has the lower ΔG^‡, so it wins.
#c020 [hx 9] Reaction
  F: Predict the major product.
  SF: [isobutylene:2-methylpropene] --HBr / --> ?
  B: tert-Butyl bromide.
  SB: [tbubr:tert-butyl bromide]
  N: Formed through the 3° carbocation. Isobutyl bromide, which would need a 1° carbocation, hardly forms.
#c021 [hx 10] Reaction
  F: Predict the major product.
  SF: [mchex:1-methylcyclohexene] --HCl / --> ?
  B: 1-Chloro-1-methylcyclohexane.
  SB: [clmch:1-chloro-1-methylcyclohexane]
  N: H adds to the ring CH carbon; Cl ends up on the 3° carbon.
#c022 [hx 11] Reaction
  F: Predict the major product. (Watch the reagents!)
  SF: [propene:propene] --HBr / ROOR (peroxide)--> ?
  B: 1-Bromopropane — anti-Markovnikov. Peroxides reverse the regioselectivity.
  SB: [bromo1p:1-bromopropane]
  N: With peroxides the reaction runs through radicals, not carbocations: a Br• atom adds first, to the CH_2 end, giving the more stable 2° radical, which then takes an H from HBr.
#c023 [hx 12] Concept
  F: Does the peroxide effect also work with HCl or HI?
  B: No — only with HBr. For HCl and HI one of the radical chain steps is too unfavorable, so they still add the Markovnikov way.
#c024 [hx 13] Reaction
  F: The alkyl halide is treated with sodium ethoxide. What forms?
  SF: [bromo2p:2-bromopropane] --NaOCH_2CH_3 / ethanol, heat--> ?
  B: Mostly propene: the alkene comes back.
  SB: [propene:propene]
  N: The strong base removes H and Br from neighboring carbons (an elimination, E2). Elimination is the reverse of addition, and it is one way to prepare alkenes.
#c025 [hydration 1] Reaction
  F: Predict the product.
  SF: [isobutylene:2-methylpropene] --H_2O, H_3O^+ / 25 °C--> ?
  B: tert-Butyl alcohol: Markovnikov addition of water.
  SB: [tbuoh:tert-butyl alcohol]
  N: H adds to the CH_2; OH ends up on the 3° carbon.
#c026 [hydration 2] Concept
  F: After the alkene is protonated to the 3° carbocation, what are the next two steps of acid-catalyzed hydration?
  B: (2) Water, acting as a nucleophile, attacks the carbocation to give a protonated alcohol (an oxonium ion).(3) Another water molecule removes the H^+, giving the alcohol and regenerating H_3O^+.
  N: Step 1, forming the carbocation, is the slow, rate-determining step.
#c027 [hydration 3] Concept
  F: Acid-catalyzed hydration is the reverse of which reaction? How do you push it toward the alcohol?
  B: It is the reverse of the acid-catalyzed dehydration of an alcohol. Dilute aqueous acid (lots of water) favors hydration; concentrated acid and heat favor dehydration.
#c028 [hydration 4] Reaction
  F: Predict the product.
  SF: [mcpent:1-methylcyclopentene] --H_2O, H_3O^+ / --> ?
  B: 1-Methylcyclopentanol (Markovnikov).
  SB: [mcpentol:1-methylcyclopentanol]
#c029 [rearr 1] Term
  F: What is a 1,2-shift? Name the two common kinds.
  B: A group moves, with its bonding electrons, from a carbon to the neighboring carbocation carbon.• Hydride shift (H:^–)• Methanide (methyl) shift (CH_3:^–)
#c030 [rearr 2] Concept
  F: Why do carbocations rearrange?
  B: To become a more stable carbocation, for example 2° → 3°.
#c031 [rearr 3] Reaction
  F: Predict the major product.
  SF: [dmbutene:3,3-dimethyl-1-butene] --HBr / --> ?
  B: 2-Bromo-2,3-dimethylbutane.
  SB: [brdmb:2-bromo-2,3-dimethylbutane]
  N: A 2° carbocation forms first. A methyl shift from the neighboring carbon turns it into a 3° carbocation, which Br^– then captures.
#c032 [rearr 4] Reaction
  F: Predict the major product.
  SF: [mbutene:3-methyl-1-butene] --HBr / --> ?
  B: 2-Bromo-2-methylbutane.
  SB: [brmb:2-bromo-2-methylbutane]
  N: The 2° carbocation undergoes a hydride shift to give a 3° carbocation, which Br^– captures.
#c033 [rearr 5] Reaction
  F: Predict the major product.
  SF: [dmbutene:3,3-dimethyl-1-butene] --H_3O^+ / --> ?
  B: 2,3-Dimethyl-2-butanol.
  SB: [dmb2ol:2,3-dimethyl-2-butanol]
  N: Same methyl shift as with HBr. Hydration goes through a free carbocation too, so water captures the rearranged 3° cation.
#c034 [rearr 6] Reaction
  F: Predict the major product.
  SF: [mbutene:3-methyl-1-butene] --H_3O^+ / --> ?
  B: 2-Methyl-2-butanol.
  SB: [mb2ol:2-methyl-2-butanol]
  N: A hydride shift turns the 2° carbocation into a 3° one before water attacks.
#c035 [rearr 7] Concept
  F: How can you spot a reaction where a rearrangement is likely?
  B: Draw the first carbocation. If the neighboring carbon is more substituted and carries an H or CH_3 that can move, expect a 1,2-shift to the more stable cation.
  N: Typical case: a C=C right next to a branch point, as in 3-methyl-1-butene or 3,3-dimethyl-1-butene.
#c036 [oxymerc 1] Reaction
  F: Predict the product.
  SF: [dmbutene:3,3-dimethyl-1-butene] --(1) Hg(OAc)_2, THF–H_2O / (2) NaBH_4, OH^–--> ?
  B: 3,3-Dimethyl-2-butanol — Markovnikov, with no methyl shift.
  SB: [dmb2ol33:3,3-dimethyl-2-butanol]
  N: Compare: acid-catalyzed hydration of the same alkene rearranges to 2,3-dimethyl-2-butanol.
#c037 [oxymerc 2] Concept
  F: Why use oxymercuration–demercuration instead of acid-catalyzed hydration?
  B: It gives the Markovnikov alcohol without carbocation rearrangements, under mild conditions.
#c038 [oxymerc 3] Reagent
  F: In oxymercuration–demercuration, what does step 2 (NaBH_4, OH^–) do?
  B: Demercuration: NaBH_4 replaces the –HgOAc group with an H.
#c039 [oxymerc 4] Concept ENRICH
  F: Why doesn’t oxymercuration rearrange?
  B: The alkene attacks ^+HgOAc to form a mercury-bridged carbocation (mercurinium ion). The positive charge is shared between Hg and the more substituted carbon: enough δ+ there to direct water to it (Markovnikov), but not enough for a free carbocation that could rearrange.
#c040 [oxymerc 5] Reaction
  F: Predict the product.
  SF: [mchex:1-methylcyclohexene] --(1) Hg(OAc)_2, THF–H_2O / (2) NaBH_4, OH^–--> ?
  B: 1-Methylcyclohexanol.
  SB: [mchexol:1-methylcyclohexanol]
#c041 [hydrob 1] Term
  F: What does the name hydroboration tell you?
  B: H and B (from BH_3) add across the C=C bond. A second step with H_2O_2 and HO^– then replaces the boron with OH.
#c042 [hydrob 2] Reaction
  F: Predict the product.
  SF: [propene:propene] --(1) BH_3·THF / (2) H_2O_2, HO^–--> ?
  B: 1-Propanol — anti-Markovnikov.
  SB: [propanol:1-propanol]
  N: The OH ends up on the less substituted carbon.
#c043 [hydrob 3] Reaction
  F: Predict the product. Show the stereochemistry.
  SF: [mcpent:1-methylcyclopentene] --(1) BH_3·THF / (2) H_2O_2, HO^–--> ?
  B: trans-2-Methylcyclopentanol (+ enantiomer).
  SB: [tmcpol:trans-2-methylcyclopentanol] "+ enantiomer"
  N: H and B add syn, with B on the less substituted carbon. Oxidation replaces B with OH with retention of configuration. Since the new H and OH are syn, the OH ends up trans to CH_3.
  3D: tmcpol trans-2-methylcyclopentanol: OH and CH₃ on opposite faces None
#c044 [hydrob 4] Concept
  F: Summarize hydroboration–oxidation: regiochemistry, stereochemistry, rearrangements?
  B: Anti-Markovnikov · syn addition of H and OH · no rearrangements (no carbocation forms).
#c045 [hydrob 5] Concept
  F: Why is BH_3 used as a complex with THF?
  B: Free BH_3 exists as diborane, B_2H_6, a toxic gas. The oxygen of THF donates an electron pair to boron (a Lewis acid–base complex), giving the convenient reagent BH_3·THF.
#c046 [hydrob 6] Concept ENRICH
  F: Why does boron add to the less substituted carbon?
  B: B and H add in one concerted, four-center step on the same face. Boron bonds to the less hindered carbon, and in the transition state a partial positive charge builds on the more substituted carbon, which receives the H.
#c047 [hydrob 7] Reagent
  F: Which reagents accomplish this conversion?
  SF: [isobutylene:2-methylpropene] --? / --> [ibuoh:2-methyl-1-propanol]
  B: (1) BH_3·THF  (2) H_2O_2, HO^–
  N: OH on the less substituted carbon means anti-Markovnikov hydration: hydroboration–oxidation.
#c048 [halogen 1] Reaction
  F: Predict the product. Show the stereochemistry.

  B: trans-1,2-Dibromocyclohexane, as a racemic mixture.
  SB: [tdbch:trans-1,2-dibromocyclohexane] "+ enantiomer"
  N: Anti addition: the two Br atoms add to opposite faces.
#c049 [halogen 2] Concept
  F: Cl_2 and Br_2 add to alkenes. Why aren’t F_2 and I_2 used?
  B: F_2 reacts with explosive violence. I_2 addition is endothermic: vicinal diiodides fall apart back to the alkene and I_2.
#c050 [halogen 3] Term
  F: Define vicinal and geminal.
  B: Vicinal (vic): on neighboring carbons, as in a 1,2-dibromide.Geminal (gem): both on the same carbon.
#c051 [halogen 4] Concept
  F: Br_2 in CCl_4 is added to an unknown in the dark, and the red-brown color disappears quickly. What does that tell you?
  B: An alkene (or alkyne) is present. Br_2 added to it, forming a colorless vic-dibromide.
  N: Alkanes don’t react with bromine in the dark.
#c052 [halogen 5] Concept
  F: Br_2 is nonpolar. How does it react with an alkene? (two steps)
  B: The approaching π electrons polarize Br_2.(1) The alkene attacks the near Br, displacing Br^– and forming a cyclic bromonium ion.(2) Br^– attacks one carbon from the back side (S_N2-like), opening the ring to give the vic-dibromide.
#c053 [halogen 6] Concept
  F: What is the evidence for a bromonium ion instead of an open carbocation?
  B: The stereochemistry: only anti addition is seen; cyclic alkenes give only trans dibromides. An open carbocation could be attacked from either face, giving both cis and trans products.

#c057 [halohyd 1] Term
  F: What is a halohydrin?
  B: A compound with a halogen and an OH on neighboring carbons. It forms when X_2 adds to an alkene in water.
  SB: [bromoethanol:2-bromoethanol]
#c058 [halohyd 2] Concept
  F: Halogenation in water gives mainly the halohydrin, not the dihalide. Why?
  B: Water acts as the nucleophile that opens the halonium ion. It is present in large excess, so it outcompetes the X^– ions.
#c059 [halohyd 3] Reaction

#c060 [halohyd 4] Concept
  F: Unsymmetrical alkene + Br_2/H_2O: which carbon gets the OH, and why?
  B: The more substituted carbon. In the bromonium ion it carries more of the δ+ charge, so water attacks there. The Br ends up on the carbon with more hydrogens (Markovnikov).

#c062 [epox 1] Term
  F: What is an epoxide? What is another name for it?
  B: A three-membered cyclic ether (a C–C–O ring). Epoxides are also called oxiranes.

#c069 [epox 8] Concept ENRICH

#c081 [cleave 11] Concept
  F: How does ozone cleave a C=C bond?

#c082 [carbene 1] Term
  F: What is a carbene?
  B: A neutral, divalent carbon: two bonds plus a lone pair (six valence electrons), for example :CH_2, methylene. Carbenes are highly reactive.
#c083 [carbene 2] Concept
  F: How is methylene (:CH_2) made from diazomethane?
  SF: [diazomethane:diazomethane] --heat or light / --> ?
  B: Heat or light splits diazomethane into :CH_2 and N_2. Loss of the very stable N_2 gas drives the reaction.

#c085 [carbene 4] Concept
  F: How is dichlorocarbene (:CCl_2) made?
  SF: [chcl3:chloroform] --R–O^– K^+ / --> ?
  B: A strong base (e.g., an alkoxide R–O^–K^+) removes H^+ from chloroform to give ^–:CCl_3, which then loses Cl^– to give :CCl_2.

  F: What is a carbenoid? Give the Simmons–Smith example.

#c089 [review 1] Big picture
  F: Which alkene additions are anti?
  B: X_2 addition (Br_2, Cl_2) and halohydrin formation (X_2/H_2O). Both go through a cyclic halonium ion.
#c090 [review 2] Big picture
  F: Which alkene additions are syn?
  B: Catalytic hydrogenation · hydroboration–oxidation · epoxidation · OsO_4 or cold KMnO_4 dihydroxylation · carbene and Simmons–Smith cyclopropanation
#c091 [review 3] Big picture
  F: Which reactions can give rearranged products?
  B: The ones with free carbocations: HX addition (no peroxides) and acid-catalyzed hydration.
  N: Oxymercuration, hydroboration and X_2 addition do not rearrange.
#c092 [review 4] Big picture
  F: Which reactions give anti-Markovnikov products?
  B: HBr with peroxides (ROOR) and hydroboration–oxidation.
